Privacy

Periplous collects nothing. There is no account, no server of ours, and no analytics. This page explains what the app touches and where everything lives, which is: on your device.

Last updated 3 September 2026.

The short version. Periplous reads the date and location recorded beside your photographs so it can work out where you have been. It never uploads a photograph. It never sends your travel history anywhere. Nothing about you reaches us, because there is nowhere for it to reach.

What the app reads

With your permission, Periplous reads your photo library's metadata: the date each photograph was taken, the coordinates recorded beside it if there are any, and the model of the camera or phone that took it. The camera model is used for one purpose — telling your own photographs from those of somebody who was handed your old phone, so their holidays do not become yours.

Periplous does not analyse the contents of your pictures. Nothing is recognised, labelled, classified or described. The only pixels it ever reads are a handful of thumbnails per trip, on your device, to work out roughly what colour that trip was; the result is a few colour values and the pixels are discarded.

Where it is kept

Your travels, the places you mean to go, and any corrections you make are stored in the app's own database on your device.

If you are signed in to iCloud, that database is mirrored to your private iCloud database, so your travels follow you to your other devices. That is Apple's CloudKit, inside your own iCloud account. We have no access to it, and neither has anyone else. It is governed by Apple's privacy policy. Nothing is stored on any server operated by us, because we operate none.

What leaves your device

Three things, and nothing else:

  • The iCloud mirror described above, if you are signed in to iCloud — to your own private database.
  • Purchases. If you buy Periplous Pro, the purchase is handled entirely by Apple's App Store. We never see your name, your email or your payment details; the app only asks Apple whether a purchase is currently active.
  • Place names you ask for. Periplous ships its own offline atlas and uses it for everything by default. When you open a particular stop, it may ask Apple's geocoder to name that one place more precisely. That sends a coordinate to Apple, and nothing else — no identity, no history, no photographs. It happens only for stops you actually open.

Anything you choose to share — an exported file, or a picture of your globe — goes where you send it, when you send it, and not before.

What we collect

Nothing. There is no analytics SDK, no advertising identifier, no crash reporter of ours, no tracking of any kind, and no third-party code in the app that talks to a network. We cannot tell how many people use Periplous, what they do in it, or where they have been.

Children

Periplous is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone, of any age.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the updated version will appear on this page with a new date. A change that affected what leaves your device would also be described in the app's release notes.
