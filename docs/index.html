περίπλους Periplous · for iPhone and iPad

Your photos already know where you went

Every trip you have taken is sitting in your library as a date and a place. Periplous reads them and gives them back as a timeline of your travels and a map of everywhere you have been.

The strata spine: one band per trip, as tall as the trip was long, in a colour taken from that trip's own photographs.

There is nothing to fill in. No trip to name, no date to enter, no account to make. You give Periplous access to your photo library and it works out where you have been, from the first geotagged photograph you ever took to the one you took last week.

What it gives you

  • A timeline of every trip, with the years down its leading edge as a coloured spine.
  • Colour that comes from you. Periplous has no palette of its own — every colour in it is sampled from your own photographs, so a week in Iceland and a week in Morocco do not look alike.
  • A map, and a real globe. Turn it, tap a country, see when you were there. Share a picture of your world that nobody else's looks like.
  • The places you still mean to go — and when your photos turn up in one of them, Periplous notices and ticks it off.
  • Your numbers: countries, cities, days away, the year you travelled most.
  • iPad properly, as a split view rather than a stretched phone. And widgets, for how much of the world you have seen.

What it does not do

Periplous reads the date and the location written beside a photograph. It never looks at what is in your pictures — nothing is recognised, labelled or described — and it never uploads them. There is no account, no analytics and no tracking. Everything happens on your device.

When it is not sure whether something was really a trip, it says so and asks, rather than putting a guess on your map.

The name

περίπλους — the log of a voyage, port by port, in order. The oldest surviving one describes the coast of West Africa; the best known catalogues every harbour of the Red Sea and what could be bought there. They are travel histories written down after the fact, which is exactly what your photo library is.
